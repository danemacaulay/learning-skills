## 1. Adaptive testing: how many items, and is "escalate until it breaks" sound?

### Takeaway
Adaptive assessment research supports the core idea (choose the next question from the last answer, stop early when the estimate is good enough), but it stops on a *precision* criterion, not on "the learner finally failed", and operational systems cap length explicitly; ALEKS brackets a whole course in at most 25 to 30 questions and shortened its check citing assessment fatigue. Effort measurably declines across low-stakes assessments, so the example session's late bare-option answers are the kind of data the measurement literature says should be down-weighted or flagged, not treated as equal evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Adaptive mastery testing with IRT gives more accurate mastery classifications than conventional mastery tests while using fewer questions. **[V]** — [Weiss & Kingsbury 1984, *Journal of Educational Measurement* 21(4), 361-375](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3984.1984.tb01040.x)
- Simulation of CAT termination rules: longer CATs estimate more accurately but with diminishing returns at large item counts; standard-error termination gives short tests with high accuracy if the SE threshold is low enough; "change in estimated theta" stopping performs comparably and is less sensitive to the item bank; fixed-length CATs were slightly worse or comparable to variable-length ones. **[V]** — [Babcock & Weiss 2012, *Journal of Computerized Adaptive Testing* 1(1), 1-18](https://doi.org/10.7333/1212-0101001)
- Knowledge Space Theory models a learner's state as the subset of items they can do, constrained by prerequisite ("surmise") relations, so one answer licenses inferences about many other items; this is the formal basis of ALEKS. **[R]** — [Doignon & Falmagne 1985, *International Journal of Man-Machine Studies* 23(2), 175-196](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0020-7373(85)80031-6); [Falmagne, Cosyn, Doignon & Thiéry 2006, *LNCS* 3874, 61-79](https://doi.org/10.1007/11671404_4); [Cosyn, Uzun, Doble & Matayoshi 2021, *Journal of Mathematical Psychology* 101, 102512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2021.102512)
- The ALEKS initial assessment asks "at most 30 questions", which the authors describe as a balance between information about the student and the burden of testing; a cluster-randomized comparison over about 140,000 assessments found a neural-network-plus-KST engine improved on the KST-only engine. **[V]** — [Matayoshi, Uzun & Cosyn 2022, *Proceedings of EDM 2022* (industry track)](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/2022.EDM-industry-track.109.pdf)
- ALEKS later cut the Initial Knowledge Check maximum from 30 to 25 questions, stating that students "perform better with shorter progress tests", that shorter checks "reduce assessment fatigue", and that students on the shorter checks learn "9% more on average". This is a vendor summary flyer of the team's research, not a peer-reviewed effect size. **[V, vendor source]** — [McGraw Hill ALEKS, "Shorter, Smarter Knowledge Checks"](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/highered/documents/aleks/innovation-shorter-smarter-knowledge-checks.pdf)
- Reliability of the KST-based ALEKS placement assessment was studied by simulated retakes built from more than 700,000 real assessments, using measures borrowed from psychometrics. (Specific coefficients not extracted; see Gaps.) **[V for design]** — [Doble, Matayoshi, Cosyn, Uzun & Karami 2019, *IJAIED* 29(2), 258-282](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00176-0)
- Effort declines within a test: in PISA 2009 reading (N = 467,819; 65 countries) a decrease in examinee effort across item positions was found consistently in every country, with individual differences. **[V]** — [Debeer, Buchholz, Hartig & Janssen 2014, *Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics* 39(6), 502-523](https://doi.org/10.3102/1076998614558485)
- In a 60-minute large-scale assessment, test-taking effort "diminished considerably across the course of the test", items became effectively harder with position, and the change in effort moderated that position effect. **[V]** — [Weirich, Hecht, Penk, Roppelt & Böhme 2017, *Applied Psychological Measurement* 41(2), 115-129](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146621616676791)
- Rapid, low-effort responding can be detected from response behaviour ("response time effort"); low motivation in low-stakes tests is associated with a substantial decrease in performance and threatens score validity. **[V]** — [Wise & Kong 2005, *Applied Measurement in Education* 18(2), 163-183](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15324818ame1802_2); [Wise & DeMars 2005, *Educational Assessment* 10(1), 1-17](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326977ea1001_1)
- Contested: in a within-subject experiment with SAT batteries of 3.5, 4.5 and 5.5 hours (N = 239), subjective fatigue rose with time on task but mean performance did *not* fall in the longer conditions; trait differences predicted fatigue better than test length. So felt fatigue and measured performance can dissociate when test-takers are motivated. **[V]** — [Ackerman & Kanfer 2009, *JEP: Applied* 15(2), 163-181](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015719)
- LLM-era adaptive testing: TestAgent runs adaptive testing as a conversation (question selection, anomaly capture, conversational report) and reports more accurate results with 20% fewer questions than adaptive-testing baselines across psychological, educational and lifestyle assessments. Preprint. **[V]** — [Yu, Zhuang, Sun, Gao, Liu, Cheng, Huang & Chen 2025, arXiv:2506.03032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03032)
- LLM-era dialogue diagnosis: an LLM can tag the knowledge components in each tutor-student dialogue turn and judge response correctness (validated against expert annotation), and an LLM-based knowledge-tracing method (LLMKT) outperformed existing KT methods at predicting correctness within dialogues. **[V]** — [Scarlatos, Baker & Lan 2025, *LAK '25*, 249-259](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706501). A separate preprint proposes cognitive diagnosis directly over teacher-student dialogue using an initiation-response-evaluation structure, reporting gains on three dialogue datasets (no numbers in abstract). **[V]** — [Jia et al. 2025, arXiv:2509.24821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24821)
- Unsuccessful retrieval attempts before instruction improve later learning of the tested content (pretesting effect, five experiments), and problem-solving-before-instruction beats instruction-first on average (g = 0.36, 95% CI [0.20, 0.51]; 53 studies, 166 comparisons). So hard, failed probe questions are not wasted if teaching follows. **[V]** — [Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009, *JEP: Applied* 15(3), 243-257](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496); [Sinha & Kapur 2021, *Review of Educational Research* 91(5), 761-798](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)

### Inferences
- "Binary search with big jumps" is the same family of idea as CAT/KST and is supported in principle. What the literature does not support is the stopping rule "escalate until a strand breaks; an unbroken strand is not finished". Adaptive tests stop when the estimate is precise enough *for the decision at hand*. For this repo the decision is "where does teaching start for this goal", so a strand that holds at the level the goal requires is finished; pushing past the goal buys no planning information and spends learner effort.
- No calibrated item bank exists here, so IRT standard errors cannot be computed and no psychometric number of items transfers directly. The usable benchmarks are structural: (i) a production KST system brackets hundreds of topics in 25 to 30 items by exploiting prerequisite inference; (ii) returns diminish; (iii) "estimate stopped moving" is a legitimate stopping rule that needs no item bank. A per-strand budget of roughly 3 to 5 informative items and a session budget of roughly 12 to 20, with early stop when the last two answers did not move the floor or ceiling, is a reasonable design inference; it is not an empirically derived number.
- The example session's notes (learner asked to stop; late answers were bare option numbers with no confidence or sentence) match the effort-decline signature. The measurement literature's response is to record response position and effort indicators per item and discount low-effort responses, which the current free-prose `grounding.md` cannot do.
- Entering near the top of difficulty means the first answers in each strand are likely to be wrong. The pretesting and productive-failure results say this is not harmful to learning, but those studies always follow failure with instruction; the probe gives none ("do not teach"), so the affective cost of a run of failures is not offset in-session (see section 9).

### Gaps
- I did not extract the reliability coefficients from Doble et al. 2019 (PDF text could not be parsed here); the paper is the right source for "how reliable is a ~30-item KST assessment".
- I found no peer-reviewed study that validates an *LLM-generated, un-calibrated* adaptive probe (questions invented on the fly) against a criterion measure of prior knowledge. TestAgent and LLMKT use existing item pools or labelled dialogue datasets.
- I did not locate a verified source for the common claim that CAT's roughly 50% success rate is experienced as demotivating; that claim is omitted.

## 2. Prior knowledge: how much it matters, amount versus quality, and activation

### Takeaway
Prior knowledge strongly predicts where a learner ends up (r ≈ .53 pretest to posttest) but on average does not predict how much they gain (r ≈ −.06 with a very wide prediction interval), which means its effect depends on *what kind* of prior knowledge it is and whether it is activated and correct. That supports grounding as a step, and it specifically supports recording the quality of prior knowledge (accurate, fragile, wrong) rather than only its extent.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 8,776 effect sizes: pretest-posttest stability rP+ = .534; correlation of pretest knowledge with normalized gains rNG+ = −.059, with a 95% prediction interval of [−.688, .621]. The authors say this variability falsifies both "knowledge is power" and "prior knowledge is negligible" as general statements and call for work on mediating processes and thresholds. **[V]** — [Simonsmeier, Flaig, Deiglmayr, Schalk & Schneider 2022, *Educational Psychologist* 57(1), 31-54](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700)
- Commentary on that meta-analysis: Ausubel's dictum (prior knowledge is the most important determinant of learning; "ascertain this and teach him accordingly") is not contradicted, but whether prior knowledge helps or hinders depends on conditions such as whether it is activated, relevant and congruent with the new material. **[V]** — [Brod 2021, *npj Science of Learning* 6, 24](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00103-w)
- Review of 183 sources: prior knowledge generally had positive effects on performance, but effects varied with how prior knowledge was assessed; negative or null effects were more likely when flawed assessment measures were used. **[V]** — [Dochy, Segers & Buehl 1999, *Review of Educational Research* 69(2), 145-186](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543069002145)
- Instructional support that helps novices becomes redundant or harmful for more knowledgeable learners (expertise reversal), so instruction should be tailored to the measured knowledge level; rapid diagnostic probes (for example asking for the first step of a solution) were proposed and tested as a way to adapt in real time. **[R]** — [Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller 2003, *Educational Psychologist* 38(1), 23-31](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4); [Kalyuga 2007, *Educational Psychology Review* 19(4), 509-539](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3); [Kalyuga & Sweller 2005, *ETR&D* 53(3), 83-93](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504800)
- Readers with low domain knowledge learn more from a fully coherent text; high-knowledge readers learn more (on inference and problem-solving measures) from a minimally coherent text that forces them to generate the connections. **[V]** — [McNamara, Kintsch, Songer & Kintsch 1996, *Cognition and Instruction* 14(1), 1-43](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1)
- Instructed reactivation of previously learned information while learning new, related information improved later memory for the integrated association in two experiments with university students using curriculum material; congruency with prior knowledge also helped. **[V]** — [van Kesteren, Krabbendam & Meeter 2018, *npj Science of Learning* 3, 11](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0027-8)
- Prior-knowledge activation strategies interact with prior knowledge level (mobilisation versus perspective-taking were compared across knowledge levels). **[R]** — [Wetzels, Kester & van Merriënboer 2011, *Computers in Human Behavior* 27(1), 16-21](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2010.05.004)
- Human one-to-one tutoring's measured advantage over no tutoring is d = 0.79, and step-based intelligent tutoring systems reach d = 0.76, far below the folk "2 sigma" figure. **[V]** — [VanLehn 2011, *Educational Psychologist* 46(4), 197-221](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369); the 2-sigma claim originates in [Bloom 1984, *Educational Researcher* 13(6), 4-16](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X013006004) **[R]**

### Inferences
- The floor/ceiling bracket captures *amount*. The Simonsmeier result says amount alone is a poor predictor of gains; the Dochy result says the way prior knowledge is assessed changes what you conclude. The template's per-strand "Misconceptions" and the example's notes about "recognized when offered, not produced" are already reaching for quality distinctions; they should be made explicit fields (held-and-produced, held-only-when-cued, fragile, wrong-with-confidence, absent).
- Expertise reversal supports "build from their floors, not below them" and "do not re-teach what is held". It also supports the plan's light note about Socratic versus expository steps: the lower the floor on a strand, the more explicit guidance that strand needs.
- The probe is itself prior-knowledge activation. That is a second justification for grounding beyond measurement, and it argues for keeping a short activation step at the start of each session even if a persistent record made a full re-probe unnecessary.

### Gaps
- I did not find (in this session) a dedicated meta-analysis isolating the effect of prior-knowledge *activation* interventions with a pooled effect size; the two activation studies above are single papers.
- Ausubel (1968), *Educational Psychology: A Cognitive View* is a book; I verified the quotation only through Brod 2021, not the original.

## 3. Misconceptions and conceptual change: record them as durable objects and refute them directly?

### Takeaway
Refutation (state the wrong idea, say it is wrong, give the correct explanation) has a consistent moderate advantage over plain exposition (g ≈ 0.41; a 2024 preregistered meta-analysis also finds a consistent advantage with no reliable moderators), and teachers who can name their students' specific misconception produce larger gains than teachers who only know the right answer. That supports recording misconceptions as explicit, addressable items. The contested part is what a "misconception" is: Chi's ontological-category account and diSessa's knowledge-in-pieces account disagree on whether learners hold stable wrong theories, and the Heidegger example (answers that shift under probing, "not a stable theory") looks more like the knowledge-in-pieces picture.

### Cited Findings
- Conceptual change requires dissatisfaction with the existing conception and a replacement that is intelligible, plausible and fruitful. **[R]** — [Posner, Strike, Hewson & Gertzog 1982, *Science Education* 66(2), 211-227](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.3730660207)
- Robust misconceptions arise when a concept is assigned to the wrong ontological category (for example treating a process as a kind of matter); such errors resist piecemeal correction because the fix is a category shift, not an added fact. **[R]** — [Chi, Slotta & de Leeuw 1994, *Learning and Instruction* 4(1), 27-43](https://doi.org/10.1016/0959-4752(94)90017-5)
- Opposing view: naive knowledge is a large, loosely organized set of context-sensitive fragments ("p-prims"), not a coherent theory; "misconceptions" are often productive resources misapplied, and confront-and-replace instruction misdescribes learning. **[R]** — [diSessa 1993, *Cognition and Instruction* 10(2-3), 105-225](https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.1985.9649008); [Smith, diSessa & Roschelle 1994, *Journal of the Learning Sciences* 3(2), 115-163](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327809jls0302_1)
- Learners often form "synthetic models" that merge an initial presupposition with taught content, which look like stable misconceptions but are constructed on the spot from deeper commitments. **[R]** — [Vosniadou & Brewer 1992, *Cognitive Psychology* 24(4), 535-585](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(92)90018-W)
- Review of the conceptual-change tradition and its classroom use. **[R]** — [Duit & Treagust 2003, *International Journal of Science Education* 25(6), 671-688](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500690305016)
- Refutation text versus other learning conditions: g = 0.41, 95% CI [0.30, 0.51], 44 independent comparisons from 33 studies, N = 3,869; trim-and-fill adjusted g = 0.28 [0.16, 0.39]; I² ≈ 61%. Only publication type moderated the effect; domain, age, test timing, test format and prior knowledge did not. The authors could not analyze learners' prior misconceptions because too few studies measured them consistently. **[V, full text]** — [Schroeder & Kucera 2022, *Educational Psychology Review* 34(2), 957-987](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z)
- Preregistered meta-analysis (71 articles, 76 studies, 111 samples, 294 effect sizes, 26 moderators): a consistent, statistically significant advantage of refutation texts over non-refutation texts on scientific misconceptions; moderators neither enhanced nor diminished it. (Pooled estimate not extracted here.) **[V]** — [Danielson, Jacobson, Patall, Sinatra, Adesope & Kennedy 2025, *Educational Psychologist* 60(1), 23-47](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2024.2365628)
- Earlier reviews reach the same direction: refutational text, and text combined with strategies that create cognitive conflict, are effective for conceptual change. **[R; direction confirmed via Schroeder & Kucera full text]** — [Guzzetti, Snyder, Glass & Gamas 1993, *Reading Research Quarterly* 28(2), 116-159](https://doi.org/10.2307/747886); [Tippett 2010, *IJSME* 8(6), 951-970](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10763-010-9203-x)
- Mechanism: readers holding a misconception engage conceptual-change processing when the text activates the misconception and the correct idea together (refutation structure), and not with plain expository text. **[R]** — [Kendeou & van den Broek 2007, *Memory & Cognition* 35(7), 1567-1577](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193491)
- Distractor-driven multiple choice: items whose wrong options embody research-documented misconceptions can map conceptions psychometrically. **[R]** — [Sadler 1998, *JRST* 35(3), 265-296](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1098-2736(199803)35:3%3C265::AID-TEA3%3E3.0.CO;2-P). The Force Concept Inventory is the canonical instrument built this way. **[R]** — [Hestenes, Wells & Swackhamer 1992, *The Physics Teacher* 30(3), 141-158](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.2343497); its use across roughly 6,000 students showed interactive-engagement courses roughly doubling normalized gain over traditional lectures. **[R]** — [Hake 1998, *American Journal of Physics* 66(1), 64-74](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.18809)
- Knowing the learner's specific misconception matters for teaching: among 9,556 students of 181 middle-school teachers, on items with a very popular wrong answer, classes whose teachers could identify that misconception had larger gains, "much larger than if the teachers knew only the correct answer". **[V]** — [Sadler, Sonnert, Coyle, Cook-Smith & Miller 2013, *AERJ* 50(5), 1020-1049](https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831213477680)
- LLM misconception diagnosis, optimistic end: on a benchmark of 55 algebra misconceptions and 220 diagnostic examples, accuracy "reaches 83.9% when including educator feedback and restricting it by topic"; ratio and proportion items were hard for the model. Preprint. **[V]** — [Otero, Druga & Lan 2024, arXiv:2412.03765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03765)
- LLM misconception diagnosis, pessimistic end: across nine models (4B to 120B parameters), accuracy fell from 66% on solving the problem to 40% on inferring a student's misconception from one worked mistake and predicting their answer on a rephrased problem; supplying the student's step trace improved prediction by 3 to 15%. Preprint. **[V]** — [Chen, Liu & Sonkar 2026, arXiv:2601.03217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03217)
- "Correct answer trap": on real student responses, models miss misconceptions when flawed reasoning yields the right answer; 71% of failures concentrated in two such question types; the best model produced about four false alarms per genuine detection (detection accuracy 84% versus 57% across the two models compared). Preprint. **[V]** — [Imran & Bulathwela 2026, arXiv:2605.23925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23925)

### Inferences
- Evidence supports turning the free-prose "Misconceptions:" line into structured, durable records: (a) refutation works and needs the specific wrong idea as input; (b) knowing the specific misconception beats knowing only the right answer; (c) the refutation meta-analyses could not even analyze prior misconceptions because studies failed to record them consistently, which is the same failure mode as an unstructured grounding file.
- The record should carry a *stability* field. Chi-type misconceptions (stable, category-level, held with confidence) call for an explicit refutation plus a category shift. diSessa-type instability (the example's "not a stable theory", "walked back under probing") calls for helping the learner decide when each fragment applies; labelling a passing gloss as a "misconception" and refuting it risks arguing against a view the learner does not hold.
- The LLM evidence says: treat any misconception the tutor infers as a hypothesis, not a finding. Accuracy is materially lower than problem-solving accuracy, false alarms are common, the learner's reasoning trace helps, and a correct option pick can hide flawed reasoning. That last point is directly relevant to the example session, where "a picked option sometimes ran ahead of the gloss that followed it". A structured record should keep the evidence (the learner's own words) and a status such as suspected / confirmed-by-second-probe / resolved.
- The theory file's rule that "learner answers are signal for teaching, not graph truth" is consistent with this; the gap is that the signal is currently discarded between sessions.

### Gaps
- All LLM misconception studies found are in school mathematics with catalogued misconceptions. I found no study of LLM misconception diagnosis in interpretive or humanities domains like the example session's topic; transfer of the accuracy figures is unknown.
- I did not extract Danielson et al.'s pooled effect size.
- The Chi versus diSessa dispute is unresolved in the literature; no meta-analysis adjudicates it.

## 4. Question format and confidence: MC versus constructed response, certainty marking, hypercorrection

### Takeaway
Multiple choice and constructed response measure the same construct when built from the same stem, so MC is acceptable for diagnosis if distractors are deliberately built from known wrong ideas; but MC permits guessing, can teach its lures, and can hide flawed reasoning behind a correct pick, which is why diagnostic instruments add a reason tier and a confidence tier. Item-level confidence carries decision-relevant information that accuracy alone does not: it separates "doesn't know" from "confidently wrong", predicts which errors are easiest to correct, and identifies low-confidence correct answers that need confirming.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 56 correlations from 29 studies: when MC and constructed-response items share the same stem, the disattenuated correlation between formats approaches unity; it is significantly lower for non-stem-equivalent items, especially essays. **[V]** — [Rodriguez 2003, *Journal of Educational Measurement* 40(2), 163-184](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3984.2003.tb01102.x)
- Review of distractor research: distractor construction is the neglected part of MC item writing; distractors based on common errors or misconceptions are what make MC items diagnostic. **[V for scope; R for the specific recommendation]** — [Gierl, Bulut, Guo & Zhang 2017, *Review of Educational Research* 87(6), 1082-1116](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654317726529)
- Two-tier diagnostic items pair a content choice with a reason choice so that a correct answer for a wrong reason is detectable. **[R]** — [Treagust 1988, *International Journal of Science Education* 10(2), 159-169](https://doi.org/10.1080/0950069880100204). Three- and four-tier versions add confidence ratings for the answer and the reason to separate lack of knowledge, guessing and genuine alternative conceptions. **[R]** — [Caleon & Subramaniam 2010, *Research in Science Education* 40(3), 313-337](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11165-009-9122-4)
- The Certainty of Response Index uses a certainty rating on each MC item: wrong with low certainty indicates missing knowledge, wrong with high certainty indicates a misconception. **[R]** — [Hasan, Bagayoko & Kelley 1999, *Physics Education* 34(5), 294-299](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/34/5/304)
- Confidence-based (certainty-based) marking: automated assessment "seldom makes use of information about how confident a student is", and adding a confidence judgement reduces the need for convoluted questions designed to defeat guessing. **[V]** — [Gardner-Medwin 1995, *ALT-J / Research in Learning Technology* 3(1), 80-85](https://doi.org/10.3402/rlt.v3i1.9597)
- Hypercorrection: errors made with high confidence were *more* likely to be corrected at retest after feedback than low-confidence errors. **[V]** — [Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001, *JEP: LMC* 27(6), 1491-1494](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.6.1491). Part of the effect reflects greater domain familiarity behind high-confidence errors. **[R]** — [Metcalfe & Finn 2011, *JEP: LMC* 37(2), 437-448](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021962)
- Feedback after *correct* answers matters when confidence is low: feedback doubled retention of low-confidence correct MC responses relative to no feedback. **[V]** — [Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2008, *JEP: LMC* 34(4), 918-928](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.34.4.918)
- Errors followed by corrective feedback benefit learning. **[R]** — [Metcalfe 2017, *Annual Review of Psychology* 68, 465-489](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044022)
- MC tests have a cost and a benefit: endorsing lures can plant false knowledge (negative suggestion) alongside an overall positive testing effect; well-built MC items with competitive alternatives prompt retrieval about the alternatives too. **[R]** — [Roediger & Marsh 2005, *JEP: LMC* 31(5), 1155-1159](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.31.5.1155); [Little, Bjork, Bjork & Angello 2012, *Psychological Science* 23(11), 1337-1344](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612443370)
- Testing-effect meta-analysis: initial *recall* tests yield larger benefits than recognition tests, consistent with an effortful-retrieval account. **[V]** — [Rowland 2014, *Psychological Bulletin* 140(6), 1432-1463](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)
- Self-assessment is only moderately accurate: across 22 meta-analyses the mean correlation between self-evaluated ability and objective performance was r = .29 (SD = .11; range .09 to .63), higher for specific, familiar, low-complexity tasks. **[V]** — [Zell & Krizan 2014, *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 9(2), 111-125](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613518075)
- People systematically overestimate how well they can explain how things work, and the illusion shrinks when they are made to produce the explanation. **[V]** — [Rozenblit & Keil 2002, *Cognitive Science* 26(5), 521-562](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2605_1)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports recording, per item: the question, format, options, the learner's response verbatim, stated confidence (or "not given"), any reason given, and the tutor's judgement. Each of those is used by an established diagnostic method (two-tier, CRI, CBM), and several teaching decisions depend on the combination: high-confidence wrong → explicit refutation, and expect it to correct well; low-confidence wrong → teach as new; low-confidence right → confirm before building (the template's "fragile"); high-confidence right with a produced reason → floor.
- Confidence should be asked on every item in a fixed lightweight form (for example low / medium / high) and not merely "invited when useful". Optional confidence is exactly what dropped out in the example session's late answers, and missing confidence makes a bare correct option uninterpretable (guess versus knowledge).
- Because confidence is itself only moderately calibrated (r ≈ .29) and explanatory confidence is inflated, confidence should be recorded as evidence alongside performance, never substituted for it. The example's "held at full confidence" entries are strongest where the file also notes the learner produced the chain "in the learner's own words".
- The example's distinction between "recognized when offered" and "produced" mirrors the recall-versus-recognition literature. A floor should require at least one produced (constructed) answer per strand; MC alone can establish a ceiling but is weak evidence for a floor.
- LLM-written MC distractors are not research-validated the way FCI distractors are. Writing each distractor to embody a named candidate misconception, and logging which misconception each option maps to, is the available approximation.

### Gaps
- No study found on whether asking for confidence on every turn in a *chat* tutoring setting increases perceived burden or fatigue.
- Gardner-Medwin's specific marking scheme (marks and penalties per certainty level) was not re-read here and is not quoted.

## 5. Formative assessment and feedback: what should a per-step check look like?

### Takeaway
Feedback has a medium average effect (d ≈ 0.48) with large heterogeneity, and roughly a third of feedback interventions reduce performance, mostly when feedback draws attention to the self instead of the task. The headline effect of formative assessment is smaller than often claimed (weighted mean ≈ 0.20 in the stricter meta-analysis). A per-step check should be a quick, decision-forcing question whose wrong answers are diagnostic, followed by task- or process-level elaborated feedback.

### Cited Findings
- The founding review argued formative assessment raises achievement; effect sizes of roughly .40 to .70 are commonly attributed to it. **[R; the attribution is confirmed in Kingston & Nash's abstract]** — [Black & Wiliam 1998, *Assessment in Education* 5(1), 7-74](https://doi.org/10.1080/0969595980050102)
- Stricter re-analysis: of more than 300 studies only 13 gave usable effect sizes (42 effects); weighted mean effect .20, median .25; computer-based formative systems .28. The authors say the .40 to .70 claim "is not supported by the existing research base". **[V]** — [Kingston & Nash 2011, *Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice* 30(4), 28-37](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2011.00220.x). A critical review raises parallel concerns about definition and evidence. **[R]** — [Bennett 2011, *Assessment in Education* 18(1), 5-25](https://doi.org/10.1080/0969594X.2010.513678)
- Feedback model: effective feedback answers "Where am I going? How am I going? Where to next?" and operates at task, process, self-regulation and self levels; self-level feedback (praise of the person) is least effective. **[R]** — [Hattie & Timperley 2007, *Review of Educational Research* 77(1), 81-112](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487)
- Updated meta-analysis (435 studies, k = 994, N > 61,000): d = 0.48 overall with significant heterogeneity; impact depends substantially on the information content conveyed; larger on cognitive and motor outcomes than motivational ones. **[V]** — [Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020, *Frontiers in Psychology* 10, 3087](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087)
- Feedback interventions improve performance on average (d = .41; 607 effect sizes; 23,663 observations) but over one third *decreased* performance; effectiveness falls as feedback moves attention toward the self and away from the task. **[V]** — [Kluger & DeNisi 1996, *Psychological Bulletin* 119(2), 254-284](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254)
- Formative feedback guidelines: make it specific, elaborated (why, not just right/wrong), in manageable units, focused on the task; avoid normative comparison and person praise; novices and low achievers benefit from more immediate, directive, scaffolded feedback. **[R]** — [Shute 2008, *Review of Educational Research* 78(1), 153-189](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307313795)
- Hinge questions (Wiliam): a single check at a decision point in the lesson, usually multiple choice, answerable in a minute or two, with each wrong option mapped to a known misconception, used to decide whether to go on or go back. **[S: practitioner summary of Wiliam's guidance]** — [Royal Society of Chemistry Education, "hinge-point questions"](https://edu.rsc.org/review/assessment-for-learning-online-course/2010010.article); framework in [Wiliam & Thompson 2007/2017, in *The Future of Assessment*, 53-82](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315086545-3) **[R]**
- Prompting learners to self-explain each step improves understanding relative to unprompted study. **[R]** — [Chi, de Leeuw, Chiu & LaVancher 1994, *Cognitive Science* 18(3), 439-477](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3). Generative ("constructive") responses are associated with more learning than merely active or passive ones. **[V]** — [Chi & Wylie 2014, *Educational Psychologist* 49(4), 219-243](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823)
- Mastery-based tutoring practice: estimate the probability each knowledge component is known from the sequence of step outcomes and require mastery before advancing. **[R]** — [Corbett & Anderson 1995, *UMUAI* 4(4), 253-278](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821)

### Inferences
- `theory.md`'s "check every step before building on it; prefer apply/predict over parrot; a miss means repair before continuing" is consistent with this literature and with mastery learning. Evidence supports keeping it.
- The hinge-question model adds one thing the theory file lacks: the check should be designed so that *how* the learner is wrong tells the tutor which repair to make. When a step addresses a misconception recorded in grounding, the check should include that misconception as a live option or elicit a prediction that the misconception would get wrong.
- The grounding probe deliberately withholds feedback ("do not teach"). Butler et al. 2008 and the hypercorrection work imply the answers still need eventual feedback, including the low-confidence correct ones; the structured item record is what lets `learning-teach` close those loops later.
- Feedback wording in checks and repairs should stay at task or process level ("that step assumes X; what would X predict here?") and avoid person-level evaluation.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Wiliam's primary texts on hinge questions (*Embedded Formative Assessment*, 2011; "Designing Great Hinge Questions", *Educational Leadership*, 2015); the description above rests on a secondary practitioner source and should be checked before being quoted as Wiliam's wording.
- No controlled study found of per-turn check formats specifically in LLM chat tutoring.

## 6. Sequencing and planning: does the evidence support a strictly "minimal" tree?

### Takeaway
Prerequisite-ordered, floor-anchored planning is well supported (learning hierarchies, cognitive load and element interactivity, expertise reversal, mastery learning, coherence/seductive-details research). Strict minimality is supported only against *irrelevant interesting* material. It is not supported against a second contrasting case of the same principle: comparing cases produces d = 0.50 over single or sequential cases, and single-example learning transfers poorly. "Shortest derivation depth" should therefore mean no off-goal topics, not one example per idea.

### Cited Findings
- Intellectual skills form hierarchies in which mastery of subordinate skills transfers positively to superordinate ones; instruction should verify prerequisites and proceed upward. **[R]** — [Gagné 1968, *Educational Psychologist* 6(1), 1-9](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461526809528968). A review of validation studies of such hierarchies. **[R]** — [White 1973, *Review of Educational Research* 43(3), 361-375](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543043003361)
- Learning progressions: empirically grounded sequences of successively more sophisticated understanding used to align curriculum, instruction and assessment. **[R]** — [Duncan & Hmelo-Silver 2009, *JRST* 46(6), 606-609](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.20316)
- Elaboration theory: start with an "epitome" (the simplest version of the whole that is still representative), then elaborate in layers, with periodic synthesis back to the whole. **[R]** — [Reigeluth, Merrill, Wilson & Spiller 1980, *Instructional Science* 9(3), 195-219](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00177327)
- Cognitive load: intrinsic load is set by element interactivity (how many elements must be held and related at once), which depends on the learner's existing schemas; instruction should manage interactivity by sequencing and remove load that does not serve learning. **[R]** — [Sweller 1988, *Cognitive Science* 12(2), 257-285](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4); [Sweller 2010, *Educational Psychology Review* 22(2), 123-138](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9128-5); [Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas 2019, *Educational Psychology Review* 31(2), 261-292](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5)
- Mastery learning: 108 controlled evaluations show positive effects on examination performance, stronger for weaker students; effects vary with the mastery procedure; mastery programs may increase time on task, and self-paced versions often reduce course completion. **[V]** — [Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990, *Review of Educational Research* 60(2), 265-299](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)
- Seductive details (interesting but goal-irrelevant additions) harm learning: established experimentally and in two meta-analyses, the more recent covering 58 studies; a secondary summary gives the pooled effect as g = −0.33. **[R for direction; S for the number]** — [Harp & Mayer 1998, *Journal of Educational Psychology* 90(3), 414-434](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414); [Rey 2012, *Educational Research Review* 7(3), 216-237](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003); [Sundararajan & Adesope 2020, *Educational Psychology Review* 32(3), 707-734](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4). Presenting more material can produce less understanding. **[V, title-level]** — [Mayer, Heiser & Lonn 2001, *Journal of Educational Psychology* 93(1), 187-198](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.1.187)
- Against one-example minimality: meta-analysis of 57 experiments (336 tests) found case comparison beats sequential, single-case and non-analogous case study and traditional instruction, d = .50, 95% CI [.44, .56]; larger when learners are asked to find similarities and when the principle is provided *after* the comparison. **[V]** — [Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn 2013, *Educational Psychologist* 48(2), 87-113](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.775712)
- Comparing two cases gave a "marked advantage" in schema abstraction and transfer over studying the same two cases separately. **[V]** — [Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson 2003, *Journal of Educational Psychology* 95(2), 393-408](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393). Schema induction from multiple analogs drives transfer; a single analog rarely transfers spontaneously. **[R]** — [Gick & Holyoak 1983, *Cognitive Psychology* 15(1), 1-38](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6)
- Variability in training examples generally slows initial learning but improves generalization. **[R]** — [Raviv, Lupyan & Green 2022, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 26(6), 462-483](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.03.007). Far transfer is hard to obtain and depends on content and context dimensions. **[R]** — [Barnett & Ceci 2002, *Psychological Bulletin* 128(4), 612-637](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.4.612)
- Coherence is not monotonic in "less": low-knowledge readers need explicit connective material; high-knowledge readers gain from leaner text. **[V]** — [McNamara et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1)
- Node-link maps: across 55 studies (67 effect sizes, 5,818 participants), constructing, modifying or viewing concept maps was associated with increased retention, with effect sizes ranging from small to large depending on use and comparison. **[V]** — [Nesbit & Adesope 2006, *Review of Educational Research* 76(3), 413-448](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543076003413)
- Knowledge-component view: instruction and assessment should be specified at the grain of the knowledge components to be acquired, and the right instructional method depends on the type of component. **[R]** — [Koedinger, Corbett & Perfetti 2012, *Cognitive Science* 36(5), 757-798](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2012.01245.x)

### Inferences
- Supported as designed: prerequisite edges ("A must be understood before B"), building from floors and not below them (expertise reversal), pruning tangents "however interesting" (seductive details), checking each step before the next (mastery), a coarse map shown to the learner (concept-map evidence; elaboration theory's epitome and synthesis).
- Needs qualification: "smallest set of propositions, shortest honest path" conflates two kinds of extra material. Tangential topics are correctly pruned. A second, contrasting instance of an on-path principle is not off-path; it is the best-evidenced route to the "generative structure" the theory file aims for, and omitting it predicts recitable-but-non-transferring understanding. The plan should allow, per key landmark, a paired contrasting case with the principle stated after the comparison.
- Minimal coherence is knowledge-dependent. Where a strand's floor is low, the shortest derivation with unstated connective steps is the wrong economy; where the floor is high, lean is right. This can be driven directly from grounding.
- The plan's landmarks are coarse. Element interactivity suggests flagging landmarks where several not-yet-held ideas must be related at once and splitting them, since that is where a "single motivated step" will not exist.
- The mermaid graph is in effect a hand-built surmise relation. If each landmark carried the grounding evidence that placed it (held / not held / misconception attached), the plan could be audited against the probe and reused across sessions.
- Elaboration theory suggests one cheap addition to the plan: a one-paragraph simplest-whole statement of the goal shown before the map, which also gives the learner an informed basis for approving it.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Sundararajan & Adesope's pooled g against the primary text (publisher page required login); the −0.33 figure is from a secondary summary. Rey 2012's numbers were not extracted.
- The widely quoted average mastery-learning effect of about 0.5 SD from Kulik et al. 1990 could not be confirmed against the article in this session and is not asserted here.
- Elaboration theory and backward design are design frameworks; I found no meta-analytic test of either as a whole. Wiggins & McTighe, *Understanding by Design* (ASCD, 2nd ed. 2005) is a book and was not verified through a URL here.
- No study found that directly compares a "minimal prerequisite path" plan to a "minimal path plus contrasting cases" plan in one-to-one tutoring; the qualification above is an inference from the comparison and transfer literatures.

## 7. Goal specificity: how concrete, and "observable performance" versus "feels obviously true"

### Takeaway
Specific, difficult goals outperform vague "do your best" goals (d ≈ .42 to .80 across meta-analyses), which supports step 3 of `learning-ground` (make the goal concrete). The terminal condition "the goal feels obviously true" is the weak point: felt understanding is a poorly calibrated signal (illusion of explanatory depth; self-assessment r ≈ .29), so the goal should be written as something the learner will be able to do or explain, with the feeling treated as necessary but not sufficient.

### Cited Findings
- Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than urging people to do their best; meta-analytic effect sizes range from d = .42 to .80; "do your best" goals have no external referent; goal effects depend on feedback, commitment and task complexity. **[V via full-text search result]** — [Locke & Latham 2002, *American Psychologist* 57(9), 705-717](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705); earlier review [Locke, Shaw, Saari & Latham 1981, *Psychological Bulletin* 90(1), 125-152](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.90.1.125) **[R]**
- On complex tasks where the person lacks the needed knowledge, specific *learning* goals (discover or master N strategies) work better than specific performance-outcome goals. **[R]** — [Seijts & Latham 2005, *Academy of Management Executive* 19(1), 124-131](https://doi.org/10.5465/ame.2005.15841964)
- The feedback model's first question is "Where am I going?": feedback can only work against a clear goal and success criteria. **[R]** — [Hattie & Timperley 2007](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487)
- People feel they understand causal systems far better than they do; the illusion is strongest for explanatory knowledge and drops when they must write out the explanation. **[V]** — [Rozenblit & Keil 2002](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2605_1)
- Learners' judgements of their own learning are biased by fluency and familiarity. **[R]** — [Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell 2013, *Annual Review of Psychology* 64, 417-444](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143823). Self-assessed knowledge relates more strongly to motivation and satisfaction than to measured cognitive learning. **[R]** — [Sitzmann, Ely, Brown & Bauer 2010, *AMLE* 9(2), 169-191](https://doi.org/10.5465/amle.9.2.zqr169). Mean self-evaluation/performance correlation r = .29. **[V]** — [Zell & Krizan 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613518075)

### Inferences
- Step 3 ("ask until the goal is a specific understanding") is supported. The example goal ("one connected understanding: how Heidegger's account of tool use does the work of his phenomenology and ontology, and of the critique of metaphysics") is specific about content but states no performance by which reaching it could be recognized.
- Add success criteria to the goal: two to four observable performances (explain X unprompted in own words; predict or apply to a new case; say what a named rival view gets wrong). These double as the final check and make the plan's sink node testable. This is the standard behavioural-objective form (performance, conditions, criterion), and the Seijts & Latham result suggests phrasing them as learning goals for a novice.
- Keep "obviously true" as a necessary condition; it captures something real about integrated versus recited knowledge. Evidence says it cannot be the sole terminal test, because a fluent tutor-led derivation is exactly the condition under which felt understanding outruns actual explanatory ability. Pair it with an unprompted production or transfer check, ideally after a delay.

### Gaps
- Mager's *Preparing Instructional Objectives* (the source of the performance/conditions/criterion format) is a book and was not URL-verified in this session; no meta-analysis of the effect of giving learners behavioural objectives was retrieved.

## 8. Motivation and affect in long one-to-one probing

### Takeaway
The learner asking to stop, and answers collapsing to bare option numbers, should be read as an autonomy and effort signal that the design should anticipate. Self-determination theory and the choice literature favour giving the learner a visible budget and a real stop option; the learner-control literature says control over *content and sequencing* yields roughly zero learning benefit, so the control to offer is over pacing and stopping, not over what gets probed.

### Cited Findings
- Self-determination theory: intrinsic motivation and wellbeing depend on support for autonomy, competence and relatedness; controlling contexts undermine them. **[R]** — [Ryan & Deci 2000, *American Psychologist* 55(1), 68-78](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68). Meta-analytic support in education for the link between more self-determined motivation and student outcomes. **[R]** — [Howard, Bureau, Guay, Chong & Ryan 2021, *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 16(6), 1300-1323](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620966789)
- Meta-analysis of 41 studies: providing choice enhanced intrinsic motivation, effort, task performance and perceived competence; the effect was stronger with 2 to 4 successive choices and, notably, for instructionally *irrelevant* choices. **[V]** — [Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008, *Psychological Bulletin* 134(2), 270-300](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.270)
- Learner control in educational technology: overall effect almost zero (g = 0.05; 18 studies, 29 effects); an earlier meta-analysis of 24 studies found a small negative average effect. "Its effects are neither powerful nor consistent." **[V]** — [Karich, Burns & Maki 2014, *Review of Educational Research* 84(3), 392-410](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314526064); [Niemiec, Sikorski & Walberg 1996, *Journal of Educational Computing Research* 15(2), 157-174](https://doi.org/10.2190/JV1U-EQ5P-X2PB-PDBA)
- Test anxiety impairs performance and relates to fear of negative evaluation (562 studies); a 30-year meta-analytic update reaches consistent conclusions about its negative relation to performance. **[V for Hembree; R for von der Embse]** — [Hembree 1988, *Review of Educational Research* 58(1), 47-77](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543058001047); [von der Embse, Jester, Roy & Post 2018, *Journal of Affective Disorders* 227, 483-493](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.11.048)
- Effort decline across an assessment is normal and measurable, and persistence differs between individuals (see section 1). **[V]** — [Debeer et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3102/1076998614558485); [Weirich et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146621616676791)
- Feedback that shifts attention to the self lowers performance. **[V]** — [Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254)
- Mastery-style self-pacing has a completion cost in college courses. **[V]** — [Kulik et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)
- A production adaptive system shortened its checks citing "assessment fatigue". **[V, vendor source]** — [ALEKS flyer](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/highered/documents/aleks/innovation-shorter-smarter-knowledge-checks.pdf)

### Inferences
- A probe that enters high, gives no feedback, has no stated length, and by rule does not end until every strand fails is close to a worst case for perceived competence and autonomy: the learner experiences an open-ended run of increasingly hard, uncorrected questions. The example session's stop request is the predictable outcome, not an anomaly.
- Low-cost mitigations consistent with the evidence: state the purpose and an approximate question budget up front; frame wrong answers as the point ("I'm looking for the edge"); give progress markers per strand; offer "stop here and plan from what we have" at strand boundaries; treat a drop in response effort as a stop condition; let the learner pick which strand to probe first (a low-stakes choice of the kind Patall et al. found motivating) while the system keeps control of what is probed.
- Partial grounding should be a legitimate terminal state. A strand marked "not probed" or "low-effort evidence" is more useful to planning than a floor inferred from tired answers.
- The Ackerman & Kanfer dissociation is a caution against over-reading: fatigue reports do not always mean worse answers. But their participants were in a long, proctored, high-effort setting; the PISA-style low-stakes evidence is the closer analogue to a voluntary chat probe.

### Gaps
- No study found on motivation or drop-off specifically in LLM-led diagnostic interviews before tutoring. The inferences above extrapolate from testing and educational-technology research.

## 9. Evidence-linked implications for this repo's grounding and planning skills and templates

### Takeaway
Keep the adaptive one-question-at-a-time probe, floor-anchored prerequisite planning, pruning of tangents, per-step checks, and the learner-approved map. Change the stopping rule, add a structured item-level evidence log and durable misconception records, make the goal observable, permit contrasting cases on-path, and persist learner evidence so later sessions confirm instead of re-probing.

### Cited Findings (each implication names the evidence it rests on; full citations are in the sections above)

**Keep (evidence supports the existing design)**
- One question at a time, next question chosen from the last answer, large jumps after confident-correct answers. Basis: adaptive mastery testing reaches accurate classification with fewer items ([Weiss & Kingsbury 1984](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3984.1984.tb01040.x)); KST prerequisite inference ([Doignon & Falmagne 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0020-7373(85)80031-6)).
- Probing above the floor without warm-up questions. Basis: failed pre-questions aid later learning when teaching follows ([Richland et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496); [Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)); expertise reversal makes below-floor material redundant ([Kalyuga 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3)).
- "Build from their floors, not below, not above"; "do not re-teach what is held". Basis: expertise reversal ([Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)); learning hierarchies ([Gagné 1968](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461526809528968)).
- Pruning interesting-but-off-goal material. Basis: seductive-details research ([Harp & Mayer 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414); [Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)).
- Probing "slip, gap, or misconception" on a wrong answer, and noting fragility on hesitant-correct answers. Basis: certainty-of-response and tiered diagnostics ([Hasan et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/34/5/304); [Caleon & Subramaniam 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11165-009-9122-4)).
- A check after every reasoning step, apply/predict preferred, repair before continuing. Basis: mastery learning ([Kulik et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)); feedback effects ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087)); generative responding ([Chi & Wylie 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823)).
- A coarse node-link map shown to the learner for approval. Basis: concept-map meta-analysis ([Nesbit & Adesope 2006](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543076003413)); goal clarity ([Locke & Latham 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705)).
- "Learner answers are signal, not graph truth"; "presence in the graph does not prove the learner still holds it". Basis: LLM misconception inference is error-prone ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03217); [Imran & Bulathwela 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23925)); self-report is weakly calibrated ([Zell & Krizan 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613518075)).

**Change in `skills/learning-ground/SKILL.md`**
- Replace "Escalate until a strand breaks; an unbroken strand is not finished" with a precision-style stop: a strand is finished when (a) it holds at the level the goal requires, or (b) a floor and ceiling are adjacent, or (c) the last two questions did not move either edge. Basis: CAT termination research ([Babcock & Weiss 2012](https://doi.org/10.7333/1212-0101001)).
- Add an explicit budget: tell the learner the approximate number of questions at the start; cap per strand and per session; offer "stop and plan from here" at each strand boundary. Suggested starting values (design inference, not an empirical constant): about 3 to 5 questions per strand, about 12 to 20 per session. Basis: ALEKS brackets a course in at most 25 to 30 items and shortened further citing fatigue ([Matayoshi et al. 2022](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/2022.EDM-industry-track.109.pdf); [ALEKS flyer](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/highered/documents/aleks/innovation-shorter-smarter-knowledge-checks.pdf)); effort declines with position ([Debeer et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3102/1076998614558485)).
- Add an effort rule: if answers shrink to bare option picks without reason or confidence for two consecutive questions, switch to a constructed-response question or stop; mark those items low-effort. Basis: response-effort research ([Wise & Kong 2005](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15324818ame1802_2); [Weirich et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146621616676791)).
- Make confidence a required one-token field on every question (low / medium / high) and ask for a one-line reason on MC items. Basis: confidence separates guess, gap and misconception ([Gardner-Medwin 1995](https://doi.org/10.3402/rlt.v3i1.9597); [Hasan et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/34/5/304)); two-tier items ([Treagust 1988](https://doi.org/10.1080/0950069880100204)); correct picks can hide flawed reasoning ([Imran & Bulathwela 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23925)).
- Require at least one produced (free-response) answer before declaring a floor; MC alone may set a ceiling. Basis: recall versus recognition ([Rowland 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)); illusion of explanatory depth ([Rozenblit & Keil 2002](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2605_1)).
- When writing MC options, make each distractor embody a named candidate misconception and log the mapping. Basis: distractor-driven assessment ([Sadler 1998](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1098-2736(199803)35:3%3C265::AID-TEA3%3E3.0.CO;2-P); [Gierl et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654317726529)).
- In step 3, require the goal to include observable success criteria. Basis: section 7.
- In step 2, when prior learner evidence exists, run a short confirmation (one item per previously held strand) instead of a full probe. Basis: adaptive tests start from a prior estimate; knowledge tracing ([Corbett & Anderson 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821)); dialogue KT with LLMs is feasible ([Scarlatos et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706501)).
- Allow the learner to choose strand order; keep system control of content. Basis: [Patall et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.270); [Karich et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314526064).

**Change in `skills/_shared/folder-layout.md` (templates)**
- Add a structured item log alongside `grounding.md` (for example `probe.jsonl`, one line per question): `seq`, `strand`, `target` (what the item tests), `difficulty_rank`, `format` (mc / free), `options` with `distractor_maps_to`, `response_verbatim`, `confidence` (low / med / high / not_given), `reason_given`, `judgement` (correct / partial / wrong / dont_know), `produced_or_recognized`, `effort_flag`, `edge_moved` (floor / ceiling / none). Basis: every field is used by an established diagnostic method (sections 1 and 4); assessment method changes conclusions about prior knowledge ([Dochy et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543069002145)).
- Per strand in `grounding.md`, add: `evidence` (item seqs supporting floor and ceiling), `floor_quality` (produced / recognized-only / fragile), `status` (bracketed / held-to-goal / partial / not-probed). Basis: quality, not amount, of prior knowledge drives gains ([Simonsmeier et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700)).
- Replace the free-text "Misconceptions:" line with records: `id`, `statement` (in the learner's words), `correct_counterpart`, `evidence` (item seqs), `confidence_when_stated`, `stability` (stable / shifting / one-off), `kind` (category error / missing distinction / over-generalized fragment), `status` (suspected / confirmed / addressed / resolved). Basis: refutation needs the specific wrong idea ([Schroeder & Kucera 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z); [Danielson et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2024.2365628)); knowing the specific misconception improves teaching ([Sadler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831213477680)); stable versus fragmentary accounts ([Chi et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1016/0959-4752(94)90017-5); [diSessa 1993](https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.1985.9649008)); LLM inferences need a "suspected" state ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03217)).
- Add `## Success criteria` under `## Goal` in `grounding.md`, and carry it into `plan.md` as the sink node's check. Basis: section 7.
- Make `## Notes` structured for session conditions: questions asked, stop reason (complete / budget / learner request / effort drop), strands not probed. Basis: section 8.
- Add a per-learner store that persists across sessions (for example `.learning/learner/evidence.jsonl` and `misconceptions.jsonl`), separate from the domain graph, with timestamps so old evidence can be discounted. This preserves the theory file's graph/learner separation while ending the from-scratch re-probe. Basis: knowledge tracing ([Corbett & Anderson 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821)); knowledge at the edge of a learner's state decays faster than deeper knowledge, so recent-edge items are the ones to re-confirm ([ALEKS flyer](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/highered/documents/aleks/innovation-shorter-smarter-knowledge-checks.pdf), vendor source).

Basis: [Alfieri et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.775712) (d = .50); [Gentner et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393); [Gick & Holyoak 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6).

### Inferences
- The single highest-leverage change is the structured item log. It is the precondition for the stopping rule (needs `edge_moved`), the effort rule (needs `effort_flag`), misconception records (need `evidence`), plan auditing, and cross-session persistence. Decisions (b) and (e) in the review brief are the same problem.
- The second is the stopping rule. It is a one-line change that removes the design's main conflict with both the measurement literature and the motivation literature, and it directly addresses the one observed failure in the example session.
- The applied numbers proposed here (question budgets, two-item no-movement stop, low/med/high confidence) are design choices consistent with the evidence, not values the literature specifies. They should be treated as defaults to tune against logged sessions; the item log makes that tuning possible.

### Gaps
- There is no direct experimental evidence on any of these changes *in an LLM chat tutor with on-the-fly questions*. The implications transfer findings from psychometrics, classroom and laboratory studies, and intelligent tutoring systems.
- Evidence drawn from STEM (most misconception, refutation and LLM-diagnosis studies) may transfer imperfectly to interpretive domains such as the example session's philosophy topic, where "correct" is less crisp and diSessa-style fragmentary knowledge may be the norm.
- Items marked **[R]** or **[S]** above should be re-read at source before any number or wording from them is quoted in a final report.
